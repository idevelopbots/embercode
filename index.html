An Important Message from Governor Ned Lamont and Commissioner Antonio “Tony” Guerrera

Connecticut takes pride in its highway safety initiatives and efforts to make the roads safer for all who use them. This work involves driver licensing and ensuring that new and renewing drivers are aware of safety’s critical role when behind the wheel.

For the state’s youngest drivers, obtaining your license is a time of joy and freedom, but it comes with great responsibility. Connecticut has imposed tougher teen driving laws for 16- and 17-year-old drivers to keep you safe as you gain experience.

Remember, driving is a privilege and not a right. Please buckle up—seatbelts save lives.

CHAPTER 1: Obtaining a Connecticut Driver’s License

This manual provides information needed to drive a passenger vehicle. A non-commercial driver’s license is designated as Class D. Unless restrictions appear on the license, a Class D license may be used to operate any motor vehicle except a commercial motor vehicle.

Obtaining a Learner’s Permit

To obtain a learner's permit in Connecticut, you must meet the following criteria:

  • You must be at least 16 years of age.
  • You must pass both a vision test and a 25-question knowledge test (minimum 20 correct answers to pass).

Note: The permit is valid until you obtain a driver’s license or 2 years from the date of issuance.

Managing Your Teen’s Driving Experience

  • Crashes Kill Teens: Motor vehicle crashes are the #1 cause of death for 15–19 year olds. The first 6 months of independent driving are the most dangerous.
  • Passenger Restrictions: Enforce state laws on passenger restrictions. Crash risks increase with each additional teen passenger.
  • Night Driving Curfew: Fatal crashes are significantly more likely to occur at night due to reduced visibility and fatigue.
  • Zero Tolerance: Connecticut has a Zero-Tolerance Law for teens drinking and driving.

Driver Education Requirements (16 & 17-Year-Olds)

All 16- and 17-year-old applicants must complete a driver training program through one of the following paths:

Training Option Classroom Hours On-the-Road Training Wait Period for Test
Commercial / Secondary School 30 Hours 40 Hours 120 Days
Home Training Program 30 Hours (incl. 8-hr Safe Driving Course) 40 Hours 180 Days

Parent Training Requirement

A parent or legal guardian of a 16- or 17-year-old permit holder must complete 2 hours of instruction regarding teen driving laws and safety.

Preparing for Your Driver's Test

Things you should demonstrate and remember during your test:

  • Follow all examiner directions promptly.
  • Check surrounding traffic, pedestrians, and mirrors before moving.
  • Apply the brake before shifting into gear from "Park".
  • Maintain a safe "space cushion" around your vehicle.
  • Use turn signals appropriately every time you turn or change lanes.
  • Smoothly stop and maintain control when backing up or parking.
